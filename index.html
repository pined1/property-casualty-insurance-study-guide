Chapter 1

Basic Insurance Concepts & Contract Structure

Core insurance principles, hazards and risk, the elements of an insurable risk, and the structure of a legally binding insurance contract.

I

Basic Insurance Concepts

🎧
Listen~1.7 min

1. Insurance

  • Transfers the risk of loss from an individual to an insurer
  • Based on the principle of indemnity
  • Based on the spreading of risk (risk pooling) and the law of large numbers

2. Hazards

Hazards give rise to a peril. There are 3 kinds of hazards:

HazardMeaning
PhysicalA physical condition (e.g., faulty wiring, icy steps)
MoralA tendency toward increased risk (dishonesty, intent to profit from a loss)
MoraleAn indifference to loss (carelessness because insurance is in place)
⚠️
Moral vs. morale is one of the most commonly confused pairs on the exam: moral hazard = dishonesty/intent; morale hazard = carelessness/indifference. Neither is a physical condition — that's the third, separate category.

3. Risk

Risk = uncertainty regarding financial loss.

2 Types of Risk
TypeInsurable?
PureInsurable — involves a chance of loss only (no chance of gain)
SpeculativeNot insurable — involves a chance of gain (e.g., gambling, investing)
Methods of Handling Risk
  • Avoidance
  • Retention
  • Sharing
  • Reduction
  • Transfer

4. Elements of Insurable Risk

All of the following elements apply to an insurable risk:

ElementMeaning
Due to chanceChance of loss beyond the insured's control
Definite and measurableLoss must have a definite time, place, and amount
PredictableNumber of losses must be statistically predictable
Not catastrophicThere must be limits that the loss can't exceed
Large exposureInsurer must be able to predict losses based on the law of large numbers
Randomly selected exposureInsurer must have a fair proportion of both good and poor risks
II

Insurance Contracts

🎧
Listen~1.6 min

1. Elements of a Legal Contract

All of the following requirements apply:

ElementMeaning
AgreementOffer and acceptance
ConsiderationPremiums and representations on the part of the insured; payment of claims on the part of the insurer
Competent partiesOf legal age, sound mental capacity, and not under the influence of drugs or alcohol
Legal purposeNot against public policy

2. Contract Characteristics

CharacteristicMeaning
AdhesionOne party (the insurer) prepares the contract; the other party must accept it as is
AleatoryExchange of unequal amounts (a small premium for a potentially large claim payout)
ConditionalCertain conditions must be met
PersonalBetween the policyowner and the insurance company
UnilateralOnly one of the parties to the contract is legally bound to do anything
⚠️
Unilateral means only the insurer is legally bound (to pay a covered claim) — the insured can stop paying premiums or walk away at any time without being sued for "breach of contract."

3. Legal Interpretations

  • Ambiguities in the contract are always resolved in favor of the insured
  • The insured can reasonably expect coverage based on the agent's words and actions
  • Material misrepresentations (if intentional), breach of warranties, concealment, fraud — all can void the contract
💡
Because the contract is one of adhesion (the insurer writes it), courts resolve ambiguous language against the insurer — i.e., in favor of the insured. This is the direct legal consequence of adhesion.
III

Trap Summary

🎧
Listen~1.0 min
  • Speculative risk is never insurable — only pure risk (chance of loss only, no chance of gain) can be insured.
  • Moral hazard = dishonesty/intent to profit from loss; morale hazard = carelessness/indifference; physical hazard = a physical condition. Three distinct categories, easy to mix up the first two.
  • Unilateral: only the insurer is bound to perform (pay claims) — the insured isn't legally forced to keep paying premiums.
  • Adhesion + ambiguous wording → always construed in favor of the insured, since the insurer wrote the contract.
  • Aleatory = unequal exchange of value (small premium, potentially large claim) — don't confuse with "conditional."
  • 6 elements of an insurable risk: due to chance, definite/measurable, predictable, not catastrophic, large exposure, randomly selected exposure.
  • 4 elements of a legal contract: agreement, consideration, competent parties, legal purpose.
Chapter 2

P&C Terms, Policy Structure & Torts

Rating organizations, key property & casualty terminology, policy components and provisions, loss valuation methods, and tort, negligence, and liability concepts.

I

Independent Rating Organizations

🎧
Listen~0.6 min

Insurance Services Office (ISO)

  • Creates forms for the standard market in accordance with state regulations
  • Insurers pay for access to forms

Association for Cooperative Research and Development (ACORD)

  • Publishes standardized forms for property and casualty insurers
  • Develops electronic standards
⚠️
Don't mix these up: ISO creates the actual coverage forms insurers use (paid access); ACORD publishes standardized forms/applications and builds electronic data standards for the industry.
II

P&C Terms — Coverage & Loss Concepts

🎧
Listen~1.2 min

1. Definitions

  • Property — covers buildings, contents of a building, or both
  • Casualty — covers compensation for harm or wrongdoing to others

2. Accident vs. Occurrence

  • Accident — a sudden, unplanned, and unexpected event resulting in injury or damage
  • Occurrence — includes losses caused by continuous or repeated exposure to conditions resulting in injury or property damage
⚠️
This is a key liability-policy distinction: an accident is a single sudden event; an occurrence also captures harm that builds up gradually over repeated exposure (e.g., ongoing pollution) — occurrence is the broader term.

3. Loss

Loss = reduction, decrease, or disappearance of value of insured property, caused by a named peril.

  • Direct loss — physical damage to buildings and/or property
  • Indirect loss (consequential loss) — loss resulting from a direct loss

4. Named and Open Peril

  • Named peril — coverage for specific perils listed in a policy
  • Open peril — coverage for any risk of loss not excluded in a policy
III

P&C Terms — Cancellation, Ratings & Claims

🎧
Listen~1.2 min

1. Cancellation and Nonrenewal

  • Cancellation — termination of an in-force policy prior to its expiration date
  • Nonrenewal — termination of a policy at its expiration date
Types of Cancellation
  • Short rate
  • Flat rate
  • Pro rata

2. Ratings

  • Class rating
  • Judgement rating
  • Schedule rating
  • Experience rating
  • Retrospective rating
  • Merit rating

3. Claims

  • First party — the insured's property or a person named in an insurance contract
  • Third party — a person or persons not a party to the insurance contract
  • Subrogation — the insurer's right to seek damages from a third party, after reimbursing the insured
  • Arbitration — used when insurer and insured do not agree on how to settle a claim; sent to one or more arbitrators
💡
Order matters for subrogation: the insurer pays the insured first, then pursues the at-fault third party to recover what it paid.

4. Insurable Interest

  • Must exist at time of loss
  • No insurable interest results in a voided contract
IV

Policy Structure & Provisions

🎧
Listen~1.8 min

1. Policy Components

ComponentPurpose
DeclarationsBasic underwriting information (e.g., name, address, coverage amount, etc.)
DefinitionsClarifies terms of the policy
Insuring agreement/clauseEstablishes insured perils, parties to the contract, effective and renewal dates
Additional and supplementary coverageAdditional amount of coverage for specific loss expenses, with no additional premium
ConditionsGeneral rules and procedures the insurer and insured must agree to follow
ExclusionsDetails perils not insured against and persons not insured
EndorsementsAn addendum which modifies the contract

2. Right to Cancel

  • The insured may cancel a policy at any time
  • Must provide notice of cancellation to the insurer
  • The insurer may cancel a policy by providing advance written notice to the insured:
ReasonNotice required
Nonpayment of premium, or policy in force less than 60 daysAt least 10 days
All other reasonsAt least 30 days
🔢
Once a policy has been in place for 60 days or more, the insurer may only cancel it due to material misrepresentation or a substantial change in risk — not for just any reason.

3. Additional Provisions

Supplementary paymentsAdditional payment amounts added to coverage limits
SeverabilityCoverage applies to each insured separately
LiberalizationBroader legislated or regulated coverage is extended to current policies at no additional premium
Coverage extensionsCoverage added for loss to property not otherwise insured
Mortgagee clauseAttached to a policy to protect the interest of the mortgagee of the mortgaged property

Loss Valuation

🎧
Listen~0.7 min

Types of Loss Valuation

TypeMeaning
Actual cash value (ACV)Replacement cost minus depreciation
Replacement costCost to replace property with like kind and quality, without deducting for depreciation
Market valueThe price a willing buyer would pay a willing seller

VI

Torts, Negligence, Liability & No-Fault

🎧
Listen~2.2 min

1. Intentional Torts

A deliberate act that causes harm to another person.

  • Libel — written or printed untrue statements intended to injure a person's or company's reputation
  • Slander — oral or verbal untrue statements intended to injure a person's or company's reputation
  • False arrest — unjustified physical restraint

2. Negligence

Negligence = failure to use reasonable and prudent care.

4 Elements of Negligence
  • Legal duty
  • Standard of care
  • Unbroken chain of events
  • Actual loss or damage
  • Gross negligence — reckless behavior showing disregard for safety
  • Assumption of risk — a defense against negligence if the person recognizes the danger and voluntarily chooses to encounter it
  • Comparative negligence — the claim is not defeated; damages payable are mitigated
  • Contributory negligence — negligence falls on the party contributing to the injury
⚠️
Comparative vs. contributory negligence are frequently swapped: comparative reduces the damages owed proportionally (the claim survives); contributory places the negligence on the party who contributed to their own injury.

3. Liability

  • Absolute liability — applies to harm or injury resulting from negligence or omission
  • Strict liability — often applied to product liability cases
  • Vicarious liability — liability imposed on one party as a result of another party's actions

4. Damages

Comprised of bodily injury or property damage.

  • Special damages — out-of-pocket expenses for medical, miscellaneous expenses, or loss of wages
  • General damages — compensate for pain and suffering, mental anguish, or disfigurement
  • Punitive damage — punishment for extreme behavior, gross negligence, or willful intent
💡
Punitive damages aren't meant to compensate the victim — they're meant to punish the wrongdoer, which sets them apart from special and general damages.

5. No-Fault Insurance

  • Insurance for automobile accidents
  • Payment of loss claims from the insured's own insurer
  • Protects the insured and passengers
  • Designed to reduce the judicial impact of lawsuits
VII

Trap Summary

🎧
Listen~1.4 min
  • ISO creates the standard coverage forms insurers pay to use; ACORD publishes standardized forms and develops electronic data standards — don't swap them.
  • Occurrence is broader than accident — occurrence covers continuous/repeated exposure, not just a single sudden event.
  • Named peril = only listed perils are covered; open peril = everything is covered except what's excluded.
  • Cancellation notice: 10 days (nonpayment or <60 days in force) vs. 30 days (all other reasons); once 60+ days in force, cancellation is limited to material misrepresentation or a substantial change in risk.
  • Subrogation: insurer reimburses the insured first, then pursues the responsible third party.
  • P&C insurable interest must exist at the time of loss — no insurable interest voids the contract.
  • Comparative negligence mitigates damages (claim survives); contributory negligence places the fault on the injured party.
  • Vicarious liability = one party is held liable for another party's actions (e.g., an employer for an employee).
  • Punitive damages punish; special/general damages compensate — keep the purpose straight.
  • 6 types of loss valuation: ACV, replacement cost, market value, agreed value, stated value, salvage value.
Chapter 3

The Dwelling Property Policy

The dwelling policy's purpose and eligibility rules, its coverage forms, Coverages A–E, policy conditions, and general exclusions.

I

Dwelling Policy Overview

🎧
Listen~1.1 min

1. Purpose and Eligibility

The dwelling policy provides property coverage for a dwelling, its contents, or both. It's written solely for properties used for residential purposes:

  • Owner-occupied, tenant-occupied, or both
  • Properties in the course of construction
  • Properties with up to 5 roomers or boarders
  • Properties with up to 4 residential units
  • Mobile homes (Basic form only) if they contain no more than one apartment
  • Seasonal dwellings unoccupied for 3 or more months during a 12-month period
  • Properties not designed as farm properties
🔢
Key eligibility numbers: up to 5 roomers/boarders, up to 4 residential units, and 3+ months of seasonal vacancy is still eligible — don't confuse these with the policy's own vacancy exclusions.

2. Form Structure

Every dwelling policy form follows the same basic structure:

  • Agreement
  • Definitions
  • Deductible
  • Coverages, including Other Coverages
  • Perils Insured Against
  • General Exclusions
  • Conditions
II

Coverage Forms — DP-1, DP-2, DP-3

🎧
Listen~0.7 min
FormPeril basisPerils covered
Basic (DP-1)Named perilThe basic named perils defined in the policy
Broad (DP-2)Named perilBasic perils + extended coverage perils + V&MM (vandalism & malicious mischief) + broad form perils
Special (DP-3)Open perilBasic perils + extended coverage perils + V&MM, on an open-peril basis
⚠️
DP-1 and DP-2 are both named-peril forms — DP-2 just adds more named perils on top of DP-1's basic list. Only DP-3 (Special) flips to open peril, covering everything except what's specifically excluded.
III

Property Coverages

🎧
Listen~0.8 min
CoverageWhat it covers
A — DwellingThe dwelling and premises; must be used principally for dwelling or residential purposes
B — Other StructuresSeparate structures at the same location, such as detached garages and other outbuildings
C — Personal PropertyCan be purchased to cover personal property usual to a residence
D — Fair Rental ValueApplies if property under Coverage A, B, or C becomes damaged or destroyed
E — Additional Living ExpensesOnly available if broad or special form dwelling coverage is written
⚠️
Coverage E is not available on the Basic (DP-1) form — it only becomes available once the insured steps up to Broad (DP-2) or Special (DP-3).
IV

Policy Conditions

🎧

General Exclusions

🎧
Listen~1.1 min

VI

Trap Summary

🎧
Listen~1.2 min
  • Eligibility numbers: up to 5 roomers/boarders, up to 4 residential units, mobile homes eligible for Basic form only (no more than one apartment), seasonal dwellings okay even if vacant 3+ months a year.
  • DP-1 (Basic) and DP-2 (Broad) are both named-peril; only DP-3 (Special) is open-peril.
  • Coverage E (Additional Living Expenses) is only available on Broad or Special form — never on Basic.
  • Coverage D (Fair Rental Value) applies when Coverage A, B, or C property is damaged/destroyed — don't confuse with Coverage E, which covers the insured's own added living costs.
  • Abandonment is never allowed — the insured cannot simply hand over damaged property and demand full value.
  • Pair or sets: insurer pays the difference in value or repairs/replaces the damaged part — not the full set's value.
  • Nuclear hazard shows up as both a condition and a general exclusion — that's correct, not a contradiction.
  • The government action exclusion generally has the same carve-out as other property forms: destruction ordered specifically to stop a fire from spreading is typically still covered.
Chapter 4

The Homeowners Policy

Eligible properties, the HO-2 through HO-8 coverage forms, Section I property coverages, Section II liability coverages, and the policy's conditions.

I

Homeowners Policy Overview

🎧
Listen~0.6 min

Types of Properties Covered

  • 1-to-4 family-owned residences
  • No more than 2 families or 2 roomers/boarders per unit
  • Tenants in nonowned dwellings (HO-4)
  • Residential condominiums (HO-6)
  • Seasonal dwellings and secondary residences
  • Other types of ownership (e.g., dwellings under construction)
⚠️
The homeowners policy's eligibility rules are stricter than the Dwelling Policy's from Ch. 3: HO caps out at 2 families / 2 roomers-boarders per unit, versus the DP's up to 4 residential units and 5 roomers/boarders.
II

Coverage Forms — HO-2 through HO-8

🎧
Listen~1.3 min

1. General Form Structure

Every homeowners form follows the same two-section structure:

  • Agreement
  • Definitions
  • Section I — Property Coverage, Perils Insured Against, Exclusions, Conditions
  • Section II — Liability Coverages, Exclusions, Additional Coverages, Conditions
  • Sections I and II — shared Conditions

2. HO Forms

FormWhat it covers
HO-2 (Broad form)Named peril
HO-3 (Special form)Dwelling and other structures on an open peril basis; personal property only for broad perils
HO-4 (Contents broad form)Tenant broad form; insures personal property for broad perils
HO-5 (Comprehensive form)Covers both the dwelling and other structures on an open peril basis
HO-6 (Condominium unit owners)Expands coverage to include parts of the building
HO-8 (Modified coverage form)Used when replacement cost coverage is not practical (e.g., older homes)
⚠️
The HO-3 vs. HO-5 distinction is heavily tested: HO-3 is open-peril for the dwelling but only broad/named-peril for personal property. HO-5 is the broadest residential form — open-peril for both the dwelling and personal property.
III

Section I — Property Coverages

🎧
Listen~1.2 min
CoverageDetails
A — DwellingDirect damages. In all forms except HO-4. Covers the dwelling and any materials/supplies on or next to the premises used for construction, alteration, or repair. Does NOT cover land.
B — Other StructuresDirect damages. Basic amount = 10% of the Coverage A limit (a policy may be written with higher limits). Does NOT include land, or structures rented out or used to conduct business.
C — Personal PropertyDirect damages. Mandatory coverage in all HO forms, and broader than personal property coverage under the DP forms.
D — Loss of UseIndirect damages. Combines the DP policy's separate Coverage D (Fair Rental Value) and Coverage E (Additional Living Expense) into one coverage.
🔢
Coverage B defaults to 10% of Coverage A. If a covered loss makes part of the residence uninhabitable, Coverage D pays loss of rent and additional living expenses for the shortest time required to repair or replace the damage — or, if the insured permanently relocates, only for the shortest time required to settle elsewhere.
IV

Section II — Liability Coverages

🎧
Listen~1.3 min
CoverageDetails
E — Personal LiabilityResponds to claims made or lawsuits brought against the insured, arising from damages due to bodily injury or property damage. Pays up to the limits of liability. Basic limit: $100,000.
F — Medical Payments to OthersCovers medical expenses (medical, x-ray, dental, ambulance, and funeral services) incurred within 3 years of the occurrence. The insured or regular residents are excluded — but residence employees are covered.
⚠️
Coverage F excludes the insured and regular household residents — it's meant for others injured on the premises — but it specifically carves residence employees (e.g., a housekeeper) back in.

Additional Coverage

Paid in addition to the limit of liability (it doesn't reduce Coverage E):

  • Claims expense — pays for expenses against the insured during a lawsuit in which the insurer defends
  • First aid to others — pays for expenses incurred by the insured for first aid to a third party
  • Damage to the property of others — pays up to $1,000 per occurrence for damage to property of others
  • Loss assessment coverage — pays up to $1,000 per occurrence for the insured's share of a loss assessment charged by a corporation or association of property owners
V

Policy Conditions

🎧
Listen~1.0 min

Like the Dwelling Policy in Ch. 3, the homeowners policy's conditions are split by section:

Section I — Property

  • Insured's duties after a loss
  • Loss settlement
  • Appraisal
  • Other insurance and service agreement
  • Loss payment
  • Mortgage holders clause
  • Volcanic eruption period
  • Loss payable

Section II — Liability

  • Bankruptcy
  • Limit of liability
  • Severability of insurance
  • Duties after an occurrence
  • Duties of an injured person
  • Payment of claim
  • Suit against the insurer
  • Other insurance
  • Policy period
💡
The bankruptcy condition is Section II–specific: the insured's bankruptcy or insolvency does not relieve the insurer of its obligations under the policy.

Property and Liability (Applies to Both Sections)

  • Liberalization
  • Waiver or change of policy
  • Cancellation and nonrenewal
  • Assignment
  • Subrogation
  • Death
VI

Trap Summary

🎧
Listen~1.6 min
  • HO-4 = tenants/renters (no Coverage A); HO-6 = condo unit owners — both are the "non-owner-of-the-structure" forms.
  • HO eligibility is stricter than DP: 2 families / 2 roomers-boarders max per unit (vs. DP's 4 units / 5 roomers-boarders).
  • HO-2 = named peril. HO-3 = open peril dwelling, but only broad/named peril personal property. HO-5 = open peril for both dwelling and personal property (broadest). HO-8 = for older homes where replacement cost isn't practical.
  • Coverage A never covers land, and doesn't exist on the HO-4 (renters don't own the structure).
  • Coverage B defaults to 10% of Coverage A.
  • Coverage C is mandatory on every HO form — unlike the DP policy, where personal property coverage is optional.
  • Coverage D (Loss of Use) merges what the DP policy splits into two separate coverages (Fair Rental Value + Additional Living Expenses).
  • Coverage E (Personal Liability) basic limit = $100,000; Coverage F (Medical Payments) window = 3 years from occurrence, and excludes the insured/residents but covers residence employees.
  • Additional Coverage amounts ($1,000 property damage, $1,000 loss assessment) are paid on top of the liability limit, not deducted from it.
  • Conditions split the same way as the DP policy: Section I (property-specific), Section II (liability-specific, including the liability-only bankruptcy condition), and a shared Property and Liability set.

II

Part A — Liability Coverage

🎧
Listen~0 min

Part A pays for bodily injury and property damage, defense costs, and costs resulting from settlement or defense of the insured.

Limits of Liability

TypeStructure
Split limitBroken into bodily injury for each person involved, bodily injury for the entire accident, and property damage — 3 separate numbers

Exclusions

  • Use of a commercial-type vehicle in business

III

Part B & C — Medical Payments & UM/UIM

🎧
Listen~0 min

Part B — Medical Payments

  • Pays for necessary medical and funeral expenses caused by an accident and sustained by the insured
  • Expenses must be rendered within 3 years of the accident
  • Payments available from liability or uninsured motorist coverage reduce the amount of medical payments coverage

Part C — Uninsured and Underinsured Motorist

CoverageApplies when…
Uninsured motorist (UM)Bodily injuries are caused by another motorist who does not have the required liability insurance coverage
Underinsured motorist (UIM)Bodily injuries are caused by another motorist with insufficient coverage
🔢
UIM coverage = the insured's UIM limit minus the at-fault driver's bodily injury limit — it fills the gap, it doesn't stack fully on top.
IV

Part D — Damage to Your Auto

🎧
Listen~0 min
  • Includes collision and other-than-collision coverages
  • Pays for direct and accidental loss to a covered or nonowned auto

Transportation Expenses

  • Paid if the loss is caused by collision or other-than-collision
  • Applies to owned autos
  • 24-hour waiting period for expense payments for losses other than theft
  • 48-hour waiting period for expense payments resulting from theft
🔢
Don't swap these: 24 hours for most losses, 48 hours specifically for theft.
V

Part E & F — Duties & General Provisions

🎧
Listen~0 min

Part E — Duties After an Accident or Loss

  • Prompt notification of loss
  • Cooperation with the insurer
  • Forwarding legal papers
  • Submitting to physical or other examinations under oath
  • Allowing the insurer to access medical records
  • Notification of police of a hit-and-run or theft
  • Protection of property from further loss
  • Allowing the insurer to inspect and appraise damaged property

Part F — General Provisions

ProvisionMeaning
BankruptcyDoes not relieve the insurer from its obligation
Policy changesTerms and conditions may not be waived except by endorsement
FraudNo coverage to an insured who makes fraudulent statements
Legal action against the insurerCannot be brought until the insured has complied with policy provisions
Subrogation rightsThe insurer has the right to recover payments from a responsible party
Policy period and territoryThe United States, U.S. territories, and Canada
TerminationThe rights and duties of the insured and insurer to cancel or nonrenew coverage
AssignmentThe insured cannot transfer any right or interest in the policy without the insurer's written consent
Two or more policiesIf multiple policies cover the same loss, the insurer's liability is limited to the highest single limit of insurance among them
⚠️
"Two or more policies" is a common trap: the insurer pays up to the highest single limit among the overlapping policies — not the sum of all of them added together.

VII

Trap Summary

🎧
Listen~0 min
  • 6 parts: A Liability, B Medical Payments, C Uninsured Motorist, D Damage to Your Auto, E Duties, F General Provisions.
  • 4 types of auto: owned, nonowned, hired, temporary substitute — temporary substitute specifically covers a replacement vehicle while the insured's own auto is out for breakdown, repair, loss, or destruction.
  • Split limit = three separate numbers (per-person BI / per-accident BI / property damage); combined single limit = one number for everything.
  • Part A excludes: intentional injury, unauthorized use, commercial use in business, and damage to transported property.
  • Medical Payments: 3-year window; liability or UM payments reduce med-pay — they don't stack.
  • UIM = insured's UIM limit minus the at-fault driver's BI limit — it closes the gap, not a bonus payout.
  • Part D transportation expenses: 24-hour wait for most losses, 48-hour wait specifically for theft.
  • Bankruptcy never relieves the insurer of its obligations — same rule as the Homeowners policy (Ch. 4).
  • Two or more policies: insurer pays up to the highest single limit, not the combined total of every overlapping policy.
  • Policy territory = U.S., U.S. territories, and Canada — not worldwide.
  • Towing and labor costs endorsement: basic limit of $25.
